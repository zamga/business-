A sale process tests a business in a way ordinary trading rarely does. Buyers and their advisers examine the numbers, the contracts, the people and the risks, and every issue they find becomes a price adjustment, a contractual protection or a reason to walk away. Issues the seller finds early can usually be resolved or explained. Issues a buyer finds late are negotiated against the seller.

The year before a sale is where that difference is made.

## 1. Know what you want from the transaction

Price is rarely the only objective. Owners also care about timing, their own role after completion, the future of the team, and whether to sell everything or keep a stake. These priorities decide which buyers to approach and which structure to propose. Settle them first, and agree them among all shareholders.

## 2. Make the numbers speak for themselves

Buyers rely on the quality of financial information as much as on the results. Monthly management accounts, a clear bridge from reported to underlying earnings and an honest view of one-off items reduce the room for argument later. If your reporting cannot answer the obvious questions quickly, a buyer will assume the answers are unfavourable.

## 3. Reduce dependence on a few people, customers or contracts

Concentration is one of the most common reasons for a lower price or a larger share of deferred consideration. Where one customer, one supplier or one person, often the owner, carries too much of the business, a year is long enough to begin changing it and to show that the change is real.

## 4. Put the paperwork in order

Customer and supplier contracts, employment terms, intellectual property, licences and property arrangements will all be reviewed. Missing signatures, change-of-control clauses and informal arrangements are cheap to fix in advance and expensive to discover in due diligence.

> Issues found by the seller can be resolved or explained. Issues found by a buyer are negotiated against you.

## 5. Understand the value, and what moves it

A considered view of value, expressed as a range with its drivers, helps you decide whether to proceed now or later, and which improvements are worth making before going to market. It also anchors expectations before the first offer arrives.

## 6. Decide who will know, and when

Confidentiality protects the business while the process runs. Decide early who inside the company needs to be involved, how the process will be explained to them, and how customers, suppliers and competitors will be kept from learning of a sale before you are ready.

## When to begin

The best time to start is before the decision to sell has been made. An early, confidential assessment commits you to nothing and leaves time to act on what it finds.
