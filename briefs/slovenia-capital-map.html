SloveniaWhere capital goes, and where it compounds

The largest cheques in Slovenia are sovereign. The strongest returns for private capital sit in the €15–75M founder mid-market, where 1990s owners are exiting together and local buyout money is thin.

Capital poolsMoney in vs. returns out

The big money is sovereign. The returns are private.

Slovenia is a eurozone export economy that sells mainly to Germany and Austria. IMAD expects exports to recover as industry there picks up. A company bought in 2026 is bought before that recovery shows in its earnings.

The largest pools of capital are the state's: the JEK2 nuclear plant and public investment, which IMAD names as the main driver of investment growth. Private buyout capital is a rounding error next to them. That scarcity is the edge.

Play in the €15–75M band

Below €10M, you compete with owner-operators buying a job. Above €100M, you compete with pan-regional funds and German and Austrian corporate buyers. Between the two, the only regular bidders are a handful of local funds with small vehicles.

Each platform adds smaller companies across the region. A €150–250M pool, or deal-by-deal funding with investors co-investing, fits the opportunity. A larger fund forces you up into €100M+ deals, where the competition is. A 2026 tax change sets a 0% corporate tax rate for qualifying investment funds; check whether a Slovenian vehicle would qualify.

Ranked playsBy conviction, highest first

Five plays, ranked

  1. 1

    Founder succession buyouts

    Conviction: highest

    Targets are industrial exporters (car-parts suppliers moving to EV work, precision engineering, electrical components), business IT services and healthcare.

    • Buy the shares, not the assets. Capital gains tax for individuals falls with the holding period and reaches zero for long-held stakes. A 1990s founder pays nothing on a share sale. Sellers will insist on it, so price it in.
    • Employee ownership as a lever. A 2026 law creates employee ownership cooperatives with deductible contributions and better capital gains treatment. Use it to give managers and key staff equity cheaply. It also lets a family feel the company stays local, which wins deals on more than price.
    Assumption

    • Senior debt 2.5–3.5×
    • Exit 8–10×

  2. 2

    Regional buy-and-build from a Slovenian base

    Conviction: high

    Keep the holding company in Slovenia for EU law and eurozone bank financing. Buy smaller companies in Croatia, Serbia and Bosnia at lower multiples, which pulls down the group's average entry price. Selling the combined group at a higher multiple than you paid for the add-ons is where most of the return comes from.

    Proof it works

    Jun 2026
    Advance Capital Partners buys 50% of Adria Dental Group from Provectus
    2026
    ACP-backed Mass agrees to buy Leder & Schuh (Humanic, Shoe4You), a larger Austrian group. Closing expected in 2026.
  3. 3

    State asset sales under the new government

    Conviction: medium, timing-dependent

    Janez Janša returned as prime minister with a minority coalition of 43 of 90 seats, relying on two small parties for support. His cabinet, approved on 4 June 2026, runs a pro-market, deregulatory programme. SDH's 2026 asset plan was approved on 5 February 2026 by the previous government, so expect it to be revised.

    • Go for stakes SDH classes as non-strategic. Don't wait for Telekom Slovenije or NLB to come to market.
    • SDH is tendering packages of loans owed by individuals and companies, some backed by personal guarantees. That is a distressed-debt entry point.
    • A minority government can fall. Only pursue processes that can sign within 12–18 months.
  4. 4

    JEK2 suppliers

    Conviction: position to build

    The new government calls JEK2 a priority, and planning for the site, immediately west of the existing Krško plant, began in February 2026. Engineering, equipment and service firms that will win work ahead of a 2029 decision can be bought before their order books are priced in. Specific targets still need to be identified.

  5. 5

    The Ljubljana Stock Exchange

    Conviction: lowest

    Krka's discount has closed; the bank is still cheap. Use the exchange to park cash or take small stakes. Taking the big names private isn't realistic because the state holds blocking stakes.

SourcingFinding deals before brokers do

The register is the deal list

AJPES, the national business register, publishes annual accounts for every Slovenian company. Run this screen and the output is a list of likely succession targets no broker has marketed. Founders' ages aren't in the register, but a founding date in the early 1990s is a strong stand-in.
